# Drop POESESSID, tame the poller, keep everything

Of the nine things your tool does, **seven are fine, one needs re-plumbing, and exactly one should be deleted** — and the one to delete is the POESESSID stash reader, because it is the only feature in the list that GGG has written against twice, most recently in a Path of Exile 1 forum thread in July 2025. The headline risk you are worried about is largely misplaced: the only enforcement event GGG has ever confirmed for a price-checking overlay was a **website account lock, not a game ban**, triggered by roughly **6.1 million HTTP 429s in a single day**, and GGG's own staff drew the threshold explicitly — "If you're not generating millions of 429's ... then you're fine" ([DevTrackers, Novynn/GGG, May 2020](https://devtrackers.gg/pathofexile/p/cdea4d78-psa-waves-of-players-being-banned-for-use-of-third-party-tool-poe-overlay)). Your overlay, your hotkeys and your clipboard import sit inside written permission, not merely tolerance: GGG's Developer Docs classify "Executable apps that run independently from the game" as "While not encouraged, these are permitted", and they explicitly contemplate reading the clipboard, bounded to "once per manual key press" ([Developer Docs, fetched 2026-11-22](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/index)). Three facts postdate every assumption in your current notes: **GGG revised the macro and overlay policy wording on 12 November 2026**, league 3.30.0 "Covens of Umbra" launched 13 November and is being rebalanced mid-league, and **Streamlit 1.67.0 (2026-11-05) fixed both of the specific frictions that drove the rewrite impulse**. Read together, those three mean the correct move is not a rewrite but a staged migration whose first two steps — upgrade Streamlit, export the models to ONNX — cost days, commit to no architecture, and may remove most of your reason for rewriting at all.

## GGG rewrote the overlay rules ten days ago, and the wording now favours you

The governing instrument for your tool is not the Terms of Use — it is the Developer Docs, and the Terms make that so. Clause 22 states that "You must comply with Grinding Gear Games' technical documentation and policies ... regarding use of the Website APIs ('API Policies')" ([Terms of Use, cl. 22](https://www.pathofexile.com/legal/terms-of-use-and-privacy-policy)). That matters enormously, because read in isolation the Terms would forbid your tool outright: clause 7c bars anyone from "Utilise any automated software or 'bots' in relation to your access or use of the Website", and 7f bars "any data gathering and extraction tools or software to extract information from the Website" ([Terms of Use, cl. 7](https://www.pathofexile.com/legal/terms-of-use-and-privacy-policy)). Taken literally, 7c and 7f prohibit any program that queries pathofexile.com, including a perfectly behaved official API client. The Developer Docs are what rescue the category, and clause 22 is the hinge. **Any compliance argument you make should be built on the Developer Docs and should cite clause 22 as the reason they bind, not on a reading of clause 7 in isolation.**

The Developer Docs sort applications into three tiers, and the tier boundary is the single most load-bearing sentence in the whole corpus for you. Websites are "Little to no risk for end users." Desktop executables that run independently are, verbatim, "While not encouraged, these are permitted. They must use a public OAuth client if interacting with our APIs." And executables that "interact with the game or game files" get: "This behaviour is strictly against our Terms of Use (sections 7b, 7c, 7i)" and "Creation or use of these type of applications will result in immediate account termination" ([Developer Docs](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/index)). Note the asymmetry of consequence: **account termination is reserved in GGG's own taxonomy for touching the client**, while the sanction for API misbehaviour is "application access being revoked" or users "restricted from further access" — access controls, not bans. Your tool is a tier-2 app. Nothing it does reaches tier 3.

Under tier 2 sit two separate and cumulative rule sets. The macro rules govern "Automation of key-strokes (or other inputs) that affect the game in any way", and require that "Macros must be invoked manually by the user (automated invocations such as but not limited to: timers, reacting to file changes, or from reading the screen are not allowed)", that each invocation has one set function, and that "The resulting function may only perform one action that interacts with the game". The overlay rules govern a different thing entirely: "Apps that use information sourced from the screen, the clipboard, or the game's logs must follow our overlay rules: You may only read this information as long as the user is aware of what you are doing with that data. Reading from the screen or clipboard may only be performed once per manual key press" ([Developer Docs](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/index)). Both close with: "Introducing features against these guidelines or our Terms of Use can result in account termination for you as well as your users."

The wording above is **newer than your project's own research**. GGG's Developer Docs changelog, under "3.30.0: Covens of Umbra" and stamped "Updated 12/11/2026", opens with: "Updated the Policy wording around macros and overlays to be clearer about when they apply" ([Developer Docs changelog](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/changelog)). Your `docs/data-access.md` is dated 2026-08-23, so **every macro or overlay quote in your existing notes is stale by one revision** and should be re-synced against the text above. GGG describes the change as clarifying scope rather than loosening or tightening, which is consistent with what the live page now shows: both rule sets sit explicitly under the tier-2 heading, which means they bind *desktop executables* and do not obviously reach a web page doing the same thing. The previous wording could not be retrieved for a diff, so the nature of the clarification rests on GGG's one-line description.

One honest tension runs through everything below and you should know about it before reading the verdicts. Six GGG support staff replies between 2021 and 2024 repeat near-identical boilerplate advising players to refrain from "anything that interacts with the game client to provide an advantage over other players or provide information that isn't normally visible" ([Nichelle_GGG, 15 Feb 2024](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3491960/filter-account-type/staff); also JC_GGG 2021, ThomasK_GGG 2024, Drew_GGG 2024). A price checker's entire purpose is to surface a number that is not visible in game. **That criterion appears in no written policy anywhere** — it exists only in support replies, and the same staff explicitly disclaim any ability to adjudicate specific tools: "we cannot comment on the legality of third-party tools, as we aren't able to thoroughly and accurately check exactly how they work." So two GGG-authored texts pull in opposite directions: the Developer Docs grant a bounded permission to read the clipboard and display what you like; the support boilerplate discourages surfacing non-visible information. The docs are the binding instrument and the boilerplate is advisory, but the ambiguity is real and does not resolve.

## The three features that need work, and exactly what to do

### (a) POESESSID: delete it, and the decision is easy because the data is reachable another way

The reason to drop this is not that anyone has been banned — **nobody has, and GGG said so in writing**: the December 2022 announcement states GGG "had not yet banned anyone" for sharing session cookies, and the only documented invalidation mechanism is the user logging out and back in, which resets the cookie themselves ([announcement 3328601](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3328601)). The reason to drop it is that it is the single feature in your tool that is **indefensible by design**: GGG has stated that sharing login information violates the Terms of Use, a named GGG web developer repeated the warning in a PoE 1 thread sixteen months ago, and the endpoint you are calling is itself undocumented and therefore squarely inside the 7i reverse-engineering clause. You cannot write a compliance statement that survives being read by GGG while that feature exists. Everything else in your tool, you can.

The sanctioned replacement does not exist. `account:stashes` is a real documented scope that a desktop app *could* legitimately hold — public client, Authorization Code with PKCE, loopback redirect at `http://127.0.0.1:8080/callback` ([Authorization docs](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/authorization)) — but it needs a `client_id`, and the live Developer Docs state flatly, under "Registering your Application": **"We are currently unable to process new applications."** No form, no email, no reopening date ([index](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/index), fetched 2026-11-22). The ecosystem's actual migration path was never POESESSID→OAuth; it was **POESESSID→no auth at all**. Sidekick, the most actively maintained peer tool (pushed 2026-11-22), states it outright: "Sidekick has no login" ([repo](https://github.com/Sidekick-Poe/Sidekick)). Awakened PoE Trade mentions neither POESESSID nor OAuth anywhere. Exilence Next is the one tool that did the officially sanctioned thing — confidential OAuth client, hosted backend at `next.exilence.app`, reading `account:stashes` — and it is the one that **has not had a code push since 2023-05-23** ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/viktorgullmark/exilence-next)). The features that needed account scope are precisely the features that died.

So keep `src/stash/clipboard_import.py` and `src/stash/json_import.py` as the stash ingress and retire `src/stash/official_api.py`. The user exports or copies; the tool parses. That is the same posture as every survivor, and it eliminates the entire credential-risk class rather than managing it.

### (b-ii) Bulk collection: re-budget it against the policy you already measured

Your own `src/market/rate_limit.py` records the observed search policy as `5:10:60,15:60:300,30:300:1800,600:21600:3600`, read from live `X-Rate-Limit-Ip` headers on 2026-08-25, with fetch at `12:4:10,16:12:300,50:300:300,1000:21600:1800`. **Your "30 searches per 5 minutes" figure is the `30:300:1800` rule, and it is real — but it is your own first-party measurement, not published policy.** The data researcher could not corroborate it from any public primary source, and GGG documents limits only as "dynamic", with the sole concrete figure anywhere in the docs being an illustrative `X-Rate-Limit-Client: 10:5:10` for a different policy. Treat it as credible instrumentation of your own account, read the real numbers from headers at runtime, and never hardcode them.

The under-discussed number in that policy string is the long rule: **`600:21600` is 600 searches per six hours, i.e. a hard ceiling of 2,400 searches per day**, with a one-hour penalty for breaching it. That, not the 30/5min rule, is what actually constrains a tool that wants to both collect data and track listings. Against it, the arithmetic is unforgiving on per-item lookups and generous on population searches. Per-item live comparables cost 4–7 requests per item, which is **4.3–7.5 items per five-minute window, or roughly 52–90 items per hour** — a 144-slot quad tab takes between 1.6 and 2.8 hours to price item by item. One broad population search plus its fetch pages yields on the order of 100 listings for a single search hit, so **a 2,000-listing training set rebuilds in about 20 searches — three and a half minutes of budget**. Spending the budget on refreshing a shared stratified comparable pool rather than on individual items is a **10–20× efficiency gain** for the full-tab workflow that is your actual product.

Three query-design rules follow directly and cost nothing to adopt: stratify rather than widen (a fixed small set of searches partitioned by map tier and coarse IQ band, cached per partition, instead of a bespoke widening ladder per item); sort ascending and read only the first fetch page, because the low quantiles are what a transactable price needs and the expensive tail buys requests and bias; and de-duplicate searches by filter hash so two similar maps in one tab share one query. Use `HEAD` as a free preflight before any burst — it reads rate-limit state without consuming a hit, per community testing ([r/pathofexiledev](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/pathofexiledev/comments/1c3bff2/understanding_the_xratelimit_response_headers)) — and budget search and bulk-exchange as **one shared pool**, because staff-sourced community reporting says they share a rate-limit policy ([thread 2079853](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2079853/filter-account-type/staff)).

The stakes here are not a ban. A single naive implementation already produces 1800-second lockouts in the wild: Awakened PoE Trade's maintainer, writing **three days ago**, attributed a 30-minute block to shared-IP CGNAT and to the browser consuming the last free slot — "Limits are set by GGG" — with no account action mentioned ([issue #1968](https://github.com/SnosMe/awakened-poe-trade/issues/1968)), and another current issue describes a single item search producing an 1800-second limit because the overlay queued a request per stat toggle ([issue #1965](https://github.com/SnosMe/awakened-poe-trade/issues/1965)). **The realistic worst case is that your tool stops working and takes the user's own trade-site access down with it for half an hour.** That is also the mechanism that generates GGG support tickets, which is the thing most likely to turn GGG's attention toward tools in the first place.

### (i) The listings poller: shrink it, and most of what it was for is already free

This is the feature to re-architect, and the good news is that a verified finding makes most of it unnecessary. **The trade fetch response exposes a per-listing timestamp at `result["listing"]["indexed"]` in ISO-8601** — confirmed in this session against your project's own live-captured API fixture, and already persisted in your schema as `indexed_at` (`src/database/models.py` carries the comment "indexed_at is GGG's own listing.indexed"). The data researcher listed this as an unverified assumption; it is confirmed. The consequence is substantial: **listing age is available inside every search the tool already makes, at zero extra request cost**, and the age distribution at each price level is free right-censoring information. Old-and-expensive is censored at that price; young-and-cheap is about to transact. You do not need a poller to learn that.

What the poller uniquely adds is the *removal* event — the moment a listing disappears or its note changes, which is the only direct signal that an ask was taken. That is genuinely valuable for the censoring correction, and the real-estate and used-car literature is unambiguous that dropping unsold listings "introduces significant bias" ([Opendoor liquidity modeling](https://www.opendoor.com/w/blog/liquidity-modeling-real-estate-survival-analysis)), with the most transferable result being that **the ratio of actual ask to model-predicted price predicts time-to-sale better than the raw ask does** ([survival-analysis preprint](https://real.mtak.hu/247298/1/epj_survival.pdf)). So do not delete the poller; constrain it.

Concretely: make it **foreground and user-initiated, never a background daemon**. Poll only while the app is open and the user has explicitly turned tracking on, show a live request counter and the remaining header-reported budget, and hard-cap the daily spend. Your current defaults — 15 minutes baseline, 5 minutes for listings under an hour old — cost 96 and up to 288 searches per day respectively, which is 4% and 12% of the 2,400/day ceiling. That is small, and the honest framing is that the volume is not the problem. **The problem is that it is unattended automated access to the Website, which is exactly what ToU 7c names.** A timer that runs only while the user is watching, with the user able to see what it is spending, is a different thing from a daemon, and it is the version you can defend. Lean on `indexed_at` for the survival features and use removal polling as a sparse supplement on a handful of the user's own listings rather than as the primary census.

## Part 2 — five stages, cheapest first, and the first two may end the argument

The dominant fact about your codebase is that **~1,030 tests across 68 test files sit on a clean pure-logic/UI split**, with `src/pricing`, `src/ml`, `src/market`, `src/parsing` and `src/tracking` already free of UI, and `src/ui` and `src/overlay` as the only presentation layers. That is not an incidental asset; it is the precondition that makes a staged migration strictly better than a rewrite. The tests cover the parts that are hard and expensive to re-derive — pricing logic, feature engineering, rate-limit policy parsing, affix catalogues, censoring and survival code — while the parts actually worth replacing (a tkinter overlay, a Streamlit page layer, a `.bat` launcher) are the thinnest and least-tested in the tree. **Any rewrite proposal must price the loss of those tests, and on this evidence there is no reason to pay it.**

The order below front-loads everything cheap and reversible. Stages 1 and 2 commit to no architecture at all and may change the answer to stages 3–5.

### Stage 1 — Upgrade Streamlit and retest the two frictions (days, zero commitment)

**This is the highest-value step because it may delete the rewrite.** Both of the specific Streamlit problems that drove this project toward a rewrite have been fixed in versions released after your `requirements.txt` floor of `streamlit>=1.40`. `st.tabs` now accepts `key`, `on_change` and `bind`: "setting a key lets you read or update the active tab label via st.session_state[key]", provided `on_change` is `"rerun"` or a callable, or `bind="query-params"` is set — with the default `on_change="ignore"`, tabs genuinely "don't track state" and every tab's `.open` returns `None`, which is precisely the behaviour you hit ([Streamlit docs, st.tabs](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/api-reference/layout/st.tabs)). And widget state now survives non-render via `persist_state` with a `key`: `"session"` means "The value is preserved for the entire session" ([widget behavior](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/concepts/architecture/widget-behavior)). **Streamlit 1.67.0, released 2026-11-05**, additionally ships "Keyed elements stay mounted when a rerun inserts content above them, so they keep their state, focus, and in-progress input" ([changelog](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/quick-reference/changelog)).

Pin to 1.67.x, replace your tab workarounds with `key` + `on_change="rerun"`, add `persist_state="session"` to the widgets that were resetting, and retest. **What this buys:** if it clears the friction, the remaining complaint is "the overlay is crude and the launcher is a `.bat`" — two narrow, cheap problems that are fixable without touching the UI stack at all. Doing the UI decision first risks spending the whole budget on the part that did not need changing.

What will *not* be patched away is structural and you should stop expecting it to be: every widget interaction re-executes the whole script; long synchronous work freezes the UI ("If a click triggers a function that takes ten seconds, the UI freezes for ten seconds", [Manning](https://www.manning.com/preview/build-python-web-apps-with-streamlit/chapter-4)); fragments do not fix an indivisible long operation, because "the entire app will wait for that thread to finish before a new rerun can start" ([Streamlit forum](https://discuss.streamlit.io/t/st-fragment-rerun-blocks-app/86360)); cold start is seconds, not milliseconds, on user reports ranging from 3–4 to 8 seconds; and there is no supported way to drive a native always-on-top window. **So the honest framing is "demote Streamlit to the analysis and diagnostics console, not Streamlit is wrong"** — it is actually good at the tables, charts and model diagnostics half of your product, and hopeless at the hot path.

### Stage 2 — Export the models to ONNX and drop the training stack from the shipped app (days, unlocks everything)

Your 800 MB packaging problem is a **dependency problem wearing a packaging costume**. CatBoost + LightGBM + XGBoost + scikit-learn + shap, with their shared numpy/scipy/OpenMP baggage, is the bulk; any packager applied to that set produces a large artifact. The fix is to stop shipping the training stack. The **`onnxruntime` CPU wheel for Windows x86-64 is ~13.3 MB** (v1.26.0, CPython 3.14), against ~207–210 MB for `onnxruntime-gpu` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/onnxruntime)), and `onnxmltools` converts from XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/onnxmltools)). Training stays a developer concern run on your own machine with the full stack; only `.onnx` artifacts and a 13 MB runtime ship. **The inference stack becomes `onnxruntime` + numpy + sqlite3 + a web framework — tens of megabytes instead of hundreds.**

One constraint will decide how much work this is, and it is specific: **CatBoost's ONNX export supports numerical features only.** CatBoost's own documentation states categorical support is an open issue and that text and embedding features "will likely never be supported"; exported models also cannot be loaded back by CatBoost, and the native `cbm` format is "usually faster" on x86-64 ([CatBoost, Apply ONNX-ML model](https://catboost.ai/docs/en/concepts/apply-onnx-ml)). Regression is in scope — the exporter emits a `predictions` output — so the blocker is feature *types*, not task type. If map name, tier or league are currently handed to CatBoost as native categoricals, you must pre-encode them in your own code, drop CatBoost, or keep `catboost` for that one model. **Pre-encoding is the right call and has a second-order benefit: it moves logic out of a library and into your own pure-Python surface, which your test suite already covers well.** Add an assertion to the suite that ONNX Runtime outputs match the original models on a held-out set, because converter and opset drift is a documented hazard and you have exactly the infrastructure to make it a non-issue.

A quiet third benefit makes this worth doing under every scenario including "change nothing": once the models are `.onnx`, **the pricing core is callable from Rust, C#, or Node**, which converts "leave Python" from a now-decision into a later-option.

### Stage 3 — Collapse the three-model ensemble into one quantile model, and fix the methodology while you are in there

CatBoost, LightGBM and XGBoost are **three implementations of the same hypothesis class** — axis-aligned gradient-boosted trees over your ~7 numeric features. On that feature count and roughly 2,000 rows they produce highly correlated errors, so the ensemble buys almost nothing on point accuracy while tripling training time, dependency weight, hyperparameter surface and failure modes, and giving you no interval estimate at all unless you train each model per quantile (nine fits instead of three). **This is an inference from shared hypothesis class, not a cited measurement** — no benchmark on small-n low-dimensional tabular regression surfaced — so settle it empirically. It costs one experiment on data you already have: cross-validated MAE on log price, three-model ensemble against one LightGBM.

The replacement is one gradient-boosted model with a **quantile objective at three quantiles, calibrated by conformalised quantile regression**. CQR fits lower and upper quantile models then calibrates them on held-out data to give finite-sample, distribution-free marginal coverage ([Romano, Patterson & Candès](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222)), and it "typically yields tighter prediction intervals in finite samples" than alternatives ([Sesia & Candès](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.05433)). Plain split conformal is specifically contraindicated here: its single global residual quantile is inadequate under heteroscedasticity ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)), and map-price noise is strongly heteroscedastic — a mid-roll map has many tight comparables, a top-roll Originator has one or none. **Calibrate out-of-time, not on a random split**: with a nine-day-old league and active balance patches, random K-fold leaks future prices into training and reports coverage you will not achieve. Hold out the most recent 12–24 hours.

Two more methodology fixes belong in this stage. First, **fit on log price and predict the 0.25 quantile rather than the mean.** A trade-search snapshot of asks is a sample of the *upper envelope* of the price distribution, skewed upward by exactly the listings that are not selling; the low quantiles are much closer to a transactable price. That is a one-line change capturing most of the censoring correction, with your existing `src/tracking/censoring.py` and `survival.py` taking it further once removal events accumulate. Second, **use monotonic constraints** — natively supported by all three boosters — so more Item Quantity can never lower the predicted price. With seven features that is the cheapest accuracy-and-trust win available, and a handful of noisy comparables violates it constantly.

Then make the comparables-vs-model switch **mechanical and logged** rather than implicit, because the correct architecture is both. Answer from the cached comparable pool by default; query live only if the partition holds fewer than N comparables, or the newest is older than the TTL, or the item's feature vector sits outside the cached partition's range — a genuine top roll. **A 5–10 minute TTL per partition is the right number** because nothing in this stack is fresher than that anyway: the public stash river carries a documented 5-minute delay and Currency Exchange publishes hourly ([API Reference](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/reference)). Display three numbers and a reason — "sells fast ≈ X (q0.25) · market ≈ Y (q0.50) · patient ≈ Z (q0.90), from N comparables, newest 14 min old" — and drive the confidence indicator off comparable count and recency, not model variance. Below some N chosen from your own holdout, **refuse to answer** and label the output an explicit extrapolation. This is strictly more than any shipped peer offers: Awakened PoE Trade states plainly "The app does not magically check the price of your item" and makes **no accuracy or confidence statement at all** ([quick-start](https://snosme.github.io/awakened-poe-trade/quick-start)), and the one learned-model competitor, poeprices.info, **is dead — its domain now 307-redirects to a GoDaddy "for sale" parking page**, observed 2026-11-22. There is a real gap to fill and no benchmark to beat, which also means you must create your own holdout evaluation.

Two data-source decisions round this out. Add the **official Currency Exchange API** as the chaos↔divine numeraire: `GET https://web.poecdn.com/api/currency-exchange`, labelled a **Public API with no scope required**, serving hourly digests with `volume_traded`, `lowest_ratio` and `highest_ratio` ([API Reference](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/reference)). It became public only at 3.29.0 on 21/07/2026, so it may postdate your notes. **It is the only PoE 1 source in this entire space that reports executed trades rather than asks, it is officially documented and open, and it costs one request per hour** — strictly better than taking the divine rate from poe.ninja asks, and the most defensible thing in your data stack. And **strike `/public-stash-tabs` from the design permanently**: it needs `service:psapi`, service scopes require "a confidential client with the client_credentials grant type", and public desktop clients "Cannot use any `service:*` scopes" ([Authorization docs](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs/authorization)) — so the door is closed twice over, independent of the registration freeze.

Finally, do not build on poe.ninja as a primary. It carries no rare-map-by-modifier data at all (`Map` examples show empty `explicitModifiers`), neither it nor poe.watch publishes an aggregation formula, and they materially disagree on the same items — so **neither can serve as ground truth to validate your model**. Its own API documentation page returned **HTTP 403 to automated fetching on 2026-11-22**, which is itself a signal about how it wants to be consumed.

### Stage 4 — Put a FastAPI boundary in front of the core (the prerequisite for every UI option)

This is the structural insight from the architecture research: **FastAPI-ising the core is not an alternative to Tauri, Electron or a native shell — it is a prerequisite for all of them.** One Python service owns data, model and pricing; one thin client owns hotkeys and the always-on-top window; one normal UI client handles everything else. Splitting frontend from processing backend is the Streamlit community's own standard remedy for exactly your class of problem ([Streamlit forum](https://discuss.streamlit.io/t/run-long-running-tasks-like-embedding-of-large-docs-asynchronously-without-blocking-ui-and-notify-user-when-finished/51036)), and the "long work blocks the UI" problem disappears because the server stops being the renderer.

The test-suite consequence is the real argument: the boundary converts your ~1,030 tests from an internal unit-test asset into an **API contract asset**. The same tests keep running in-process, a thin layer of HTTP-level tests pins the boundary, and the UI technology becomes a reversible decision rather than a one-way door. Path of Building has run this pattern for years — it tests its entire calculation engine through `HeadlessWrapper.lua` under LuaJIT, completely outside its GUI host ([CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/PathOfBuildingCommunity/PathOfBuilding/blob/dev/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Bind to 127.0.0.1 and add a per-launch auth token, which is what practitioners pairing FastAPI with a desktop shell do ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/making-desktop-apps-with-revved-up-potential-rust-tauri-sidecar)).

### Stage 5 — Upgrade the overlay properly, then decide the shell last

The overlay is where your code is genuinely weakest and where the ecosystem has already solved the problem in public, under MIT. Awakened PoE Trade's **entire** main-process runtime dependency list is two packages: `electron-overlay-window@4.1.0` and `uiohook-napi@1.5.5` ([main/package.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnosMe/awakened-poe-trade/master/main/package.json)). The native layer uses `SetWinEventHook` with **`WINEVENT_OUTOFCONTEXT`** — "Hook flag so callbacks run in the calling process without DLL injection" — on `EVENT_SYSTEM_FOREGROUND`, `EVENT_OBJECT_LOCATIONCHANGE` and friends, plus MSAA focus verification "since GetForegroundWindow can be wrong during rapid focus changes", an 83 ms (~12 fps) `SetTimer` fallback for missed foreground changes, `IsHungAppWindow` to skip ghost windows, and a UIPI probe via `RegisterWindowMessage("ELECTRON_OVERLAY_UIPI_TEST")` that detects when the target runs at higher integrity ([windows.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnosMe/electron-overlay-window/master/src/lib/windows.c)). **That UIPI probe is the real reason behind the folk advice to run these tools as Administrator.** Those ~500 lines are what make an overlay feel correct, and your tkinter version has none of them.

One Win32 detail is worth changing even if you keep tkinter. Microsoft's own documentation says hit testing of a layered window "is based on the shape and transparency of the window ... the areas of the window that are color-keyed or whose alpha value is zero will let the mouse messages through. However, if the layered window has the `WS_EX_TRANSPARENT` extended window style, the shape of the layered window will be ignored and the mouse events will be passed to other windows underneath" ([Window Features](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winmsg/window-features)). **You get per-pixel click-through for free from alpha-zero regions; `WS_EX_TRANSPARENT` is the all-or-nothing hammer.** Your `src/overlay/window.py` toggles the `WS_EX_TRANSPARENT` bit, which means the overlay is either entirely click-through or entirely interactive. Alpha-zero hit testing gives you a mixed overlay — click-through over the game, interactive on your own panels — without a hotkey toggle. Also detect exclusive fullscreen and tell the user: a layered window cannot draw over it, which is why borderless is the universal convention among these tools, and failing silently is the worst outcome.

Only now decide the shell, and the evidence points one way for de-risking and another for size. **Electron is the proven answer**: every surviving PoE overlay uses it, Awakened PoE Trade is on `electron ^40.10.6` as of five days ago, and its Windows installer is exactly **103,729,556 bytes (~103.7 MB)** ([GitHub Releases API](https://api.github.com/repos/SnosMe/awakened-poe-trade/releases/latest)) — which has evidently not hurt adoption, and is the number any alternative has to beat. **Tauri 2 with the Python core as a sidecar** is the smaller route (Better Stack's controlled measurement: Electron 323 MB vs Tauri 57 MB for comparable apps; the widely repeated "2–10 MB vs 80–200 MB" figures recur across mutually inconsistent SEO pages and should be treated as folklore), declared via `externalBin` with explicit shell-plugin allowance ([Tauri v2 sidecar docs](https://v2.tauri.app/ja/develop/sidecar/)). But choosing Tauri commits you to **porting the window-tracking native layer to Rust yourself, because no equivalent off-the-shelf crate surfaced in this research.** Combined with an ONNX-slimmed sidecar it is the only route to a plausibly sub-60 MB installer that keeps your Python core. A third option worth a spike: `pywebview`, which does "not bundle a heavy GUI toolkit or web renderer", uses WebView2 on Windows, and keeps you entirely in Python ([pywebview](https://github.com/r0x0r/pywebview)).

Independently of all of that, **replace the `.bat` launcher — it was right about the mechanism and wrong about the wrapper.** Bootstrapping a real environment on first run is exactly what PyApp, `axe`, PyCrucible and `pyappdist` all do; PyApp states "applications bootstrap themselves on the first run" and afterwards only check whether the install directory exists ([PyApp runtime docs](https://docs.rs/crate/pyapp/0.29.0/source/docs/runtime.md)). What the `.bat` lacks is a signed executable (so no SmartScreen "Unknown Publisher" wall and no antivirus heuristics on a script that downloads and runs code), a real uninstaller and Start-menu entry, locked dependency resolution, and a first-run experience that does not frighten people. **`pyappdist` v0.10.0 can produce an MSI installer for Windows** ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyappdist/0.10.0/)). This is a small, cheap, high-value change entirely independent of every UI decision — do it early, alongside Stage 2.

## What stays unanswered, and what each answer would change

Five questions went unresolved, and each one would move a specific decision.

**poe.ninja's actual terms and rate limits.** Both `poe.ninja/docs/api` and its Swagger page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching on 2026-11-22, so every poe.ninja fact in this report is secondhand, and the reported rate limits do not reconcile: one 2025 community document says 12 requests per 5 minutes, a 2026 PR describes capping at 2 requests in flight with at most two `Retry-After` retries, and a developer observed 429s on roughly one run in three at ~68 concurrent requests ([poestash-workers PR #33](https://github.com/sdelanos/poestash-workers/pull/33)). **If answered:** it determines whether poe.ninja is safe as a fallback enrichment layer or should be dropped entirely. Open the page in a real browser and record it verbatim — this is the single highest-value follow-up, and it is five minutes of work.

**Currency Exchange rate limits.** The API Reference's Rate Limits section did not come through in the fetch. **If answered:** it sets the polling schedule for your numeraire. The documented guidance to wait for the next hourly boundary implies one request per hour is intended and safe, and `web.poecdn.com` rather than `api.pathofexile.com` signals pre-baked CDN content meant to be hit by many clients — but confirm before building.

**Whether GGG ever shipped trade-site OAuth.** GGG said in December 2022 it was "working to extend" OAuth "to resources like the trade website" ([announcement 3328601](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3328601)). Four years later the Developer Docs index still lists no trade API, and no evidence either way surfaced. **If answered affirmatively, it changes the entire recommendation**: your `/api/trade/*` use would move from TOLERATED to a documented, sanctioned route, and row b-i's "formally non-compliant by the letter" caveat would disappear. Worth re-checking the changelog each league.

**Per-monitor DPI behaviour for click-through overlays.** `electron-overlay-window`'s README claims Windows 7–10 and says nothing about Windows 11 or per-monitor DPI awareness; the native code uses `GetClientRect`/`ClientToScreen`, which are physical pixels, so the DPI mapping must happen above it, unverified. Multi-monitor behaviour is equally undocumented. **This is the largest technical unknown in the plan, and more searching will not resolve it** — prototype against the real game on your own machine before committing to any shell. A day on a throwaway spike de-risks the biggest item in the whole decision. HDR is the adjacent unknown: all the evidence found is anecdotal and game-specific, and nobody has measured what a layered sRGB overlay looks like over an HDR scanout.

**Exilence Next's status.** Its repo has had no push since 2023-05-23 and the Electron/Angular attribution could not be confirmed; community reports of a new maintainer are secondhand. **If answered:** it is the only datapoint on whether the officially sanctioned OAuth-plus-hosted-backend route is viable for a hobbyist, and it is currently the evidence that it is not.

Two smaller honest notes. **No GGG statement after 2020 restates or updates the "millions of 429s" threshold**, so it is unknown whether the practical trigger is now lower — and the three self-reported permanent bans from April–May 2025 can be neither confirmed nor excluded as read-only-tool bans, because GGG never disclosed their causes, one has a browser-extension confounder, and the clearest one is PoE 2 on a cloud-streaming platform. And the researchers disagreed in one place worth flagging: a 2023-era forum quote of GGG's docs says GGG does "not support the implicit grant or the PKCE extension", while the current live docs say public desktop clients "Must only use the Authorization Code (with PKCE) grant type". **The live docs win; treat the forum quote as stale.**

## Conclusion

The compliance picture inverts the intuition that probably prompted this question. The features that *feel* risky — drawing over the game window, registering global hotkeys, reading the clipboard — are the ones with the clearest written permission, and your install scan on 2026-11-22 confirms there is nothing in PoE 1 on this machine that could even observe them: no EasyAntiCheat, no BattlEye, no driver. The feature that feels innocuous — a background timer quietly refreshing your own listings — is the one that lands squarely in Terms clause 7c's "automated software ... in relation to your access or use of the Website", and the feature you probably consider a core capability is the one GGG has written against by name, twice. **Your actual exposure is not an account ban; it is a 30-minute rate-limit lockout that also takes down the user's own browser access to the trade site**, because the quota belongs to the player and is shared per-IP. That reframing should change what you engineer defensively: a header-driven token bucket shared across every outbound call, a visible request counter, and a `HEAD` preflight are worth more to you than any amount of policy lawyering.

The deeper structural finding is that **there is no route by which a new independent PoE 1 pricer becomes formally sanctioned**, and pretending otherwise wastes effort. The only officially open bulk source is the Currency Exchange API, which cannot price a rare map; the only endpoint that can is undocumented and reachable only through conduct GGG's own text calls a 7i breach; and the registration queue that would legitimise anything else has been closed with no stated reopening date. The achievable goal is maximum defensibility, and it is a short list: use every official door that is actually open, follow every documented behavioural rule to the letter including the one about telling users what you do with their clipboard data, hold no credentials at all, keep your rate-limit discipline better than the official site's own users', ship the non-affiliation notice verbatim, and never touch the game. That is the posture of every tool that has survived a decade. Your codebase is three days of work and one deletion away from it — and the rewrite you were contemplating would mostly have destroyed 1,030 tests to arrive at the same place.
